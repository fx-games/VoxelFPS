# Translation Key — Forest to Station

Source material: `black-forest-1` ("Im Schatten des Waldes"), a German tabletop-LARP design. This key
is the Rosetta stone for every other document in this GDD — when a doc says "see translation key,"
this is the table it means. Original German terms are kept in *(parentheses)* for traceability back
to the source files.

## The unifying device: folk-naming

The source LARP names locations like "Old Pine" or "Hill with Grass" — wilderness landmarks a group of
wanderers would coin on the fly. A sterile research station doesn't naturally have names like that, so
we bake in an in-fiction reason it does: **the Fold has trapped waves of survivors for warped, uncertain
stretches of time, long enough that station signage corroded, corporate deck codes were forgotten, and
generations of stranded people renamed the place in their own oral folklore.** A hydroponics bay becomes
"the Garden." A collapsed gantry becomes "the Slope." This is why the station still sounds half like a
fairy tale — and it's a free narrative justification for keeping the source material's evocative place
names almost verbatim. Use it anywhere a translation feels like it wants a folksy name: it's earned.

## Anomaly Bleed (replaces prophetic dream hooks)

The source LARP gives several player characters a prophetic dream as their hook (a woman with a chain
weeping, a red bear calling from tree roots, a green dragon's voice). Rather than translate each dream
individually, we introduce one mechanism that explains all of them: **proximity to the Fold causes
neural bleed-through in dreams, even before a person physically arrives.** The station's temporal
distortion doesn't stay neatly inside its own hull. Every player-hook dream in [03-characters.md](03-characters.md)
uses this device — keep it consistent, don't invent a second explanation.
