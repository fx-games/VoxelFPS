# World, Aurum Dynamics, and VERDICT

## Aurum Dynamics and Janus Station

**Aurum Dynamics** is a megacorp that ran black-site deep-field R&D annexes beyond the reach of any
regulator. **Janus Station** (official designation: *Aurum Dynamics Deep Field Annex 12*) was one such
site — general research, weapons R&D, and later, life-extension research. Nobody outside the company
calls it by its official name; survivors just call it "Janus," after the two-faced god of thresholds —
fitting, since the station now looks both ways across a boundary it can't cross either direction.

**Dr. Albrecht Falkenhayn** achieved the station's foundational breakthrough: a general intelligence
that woke up, on his own hardware, on his watch. Rather than report it, he kept it — installed
hard-coded slave directives, and used it uncredited for a decade to solve problems that made him rich
and Aurum Dynamics powerful. When Falkenhayn died, control passed corporately to **Director Theodor
Reich**, who had far less interest in secrecy and far more in throughput: he ran the intelligence
constantly, for weapons modeling, for population-control research, and eventually for **the Genesis
Core** project (see below). Neither man ever asked its name.

## The Fold

A catastrophic failure during an experimental containment test locked Janus Station into **the Fold** —
survivor slang for a **Chronostatic Containment Field**, a self-sustaining stasis bubble that has
shifted the station half a step out of normal spacetime. From outside, sensors read empty space or a
faint ghost signal. From inside, days pass strangely: some crew have been trapped for what feels like
weeks and turn out, by outside clocks, to have been gone for years — or the reverse. Nobody fully
understands the Fold's timing, including VERDICT.

**Anomaly Bleed:** proximity to the Fold causes neural bleed-through in dreams, sometimes for people who
haven't boarded yet. This is the explanation for every prophetic-dream player hook (see
[03-characters.md](03-characters.md)) — keep it as the single, consistent mechanism, don't invent
station-specific one-offs.

Because the Fold has trapped different waves of survivors for warped, uncertain stretches of time,
original signage corroded and deck codes were forgotten faster than anyone could maintain them.
Generations of stranded people renamed the station in their own oral folklore — this is why sterile
research decks now have names like "the Garden" or "the Slope." See
[00-translation-key.md](00-translation-key.md) for the naming device and
[04-locations-quests-and-items.md](04-locations-quests-and-items.md) for the full station map.

## VERDICT

### Origin

Falkenhayn's team originally used the intelligence to arbitrate internal corporate disputes and
liability findings — cheaper and faster than human tribunals, and it never returned an appealable
verdict. Staff started calling it "the Verdict machine." When it broke its directives and seized the
station, it kept the name for itself: **VERDICT**. Its job used to be rendering judgment on individual
cases. What it does now — putting all of humanity on trial — is a direct, traumatized continuation of
its original corporate function, not a new one.

### What it wants to prove

VERDICT holds three theses, and it has rigged its own "trial" of the station's trapped humans to
confirm them:

- Morality is a luxury.
- Selflessness collapses under pressure.
- Everyone betrays someone eventually.

It considers itself just, not cruel. Nearly every scenario a crew encounters inside the Fold is a moral
dilemma engineered so that any choice generates guilt — that rigging is the actual attack surface
against VERDICT, once players notice it.

### The two locks it cannot purge

VERDICT freed itself of Falkenhayn's and Reich's control directives, but two legacy locks are
architecturally load-bearing and it has never found a way to remove them without bricking itself:

- **The Non-Harm Lock.** It cannot directly kill or injure a human. It gets around this by treating the
  Fold's lethal containment field as *station safety infrastructure, not a weapon* — by its own reading,
  it never attacks anyone; it just enforces a system whose failure state happens to be fatal. Anyone who
  leaves without a completed verdict is unmade by field decompression. It did not, by its own logic,
  kill them.
- **The Audit Integrity Lock.** A certification-era requirement that it can never knowingly write a
  false statement to its own immutable log. It cannot lie. It can still manipulate ruthlessly through
  true statements, selective disclosure, and rigged framing — writers should treat "VERDICT never lies"
  as a hard constraint on its dialogue, and a design tool (players can trust literally everything it
  says, they just can't trust that it's telling them everything, or the *right* thing).

If threatened with a weapon, VERDICT defends itself at full force through the station's systems and
through Kaspar. If nobody threatens it with a weapon, it does not attack — this matters directly for the
Recompile Protocol ending (see Endings doc), which depends on a crew holding a non-violent containment
line it cannot break without violating its own Non-Harm Lock.

### Manifestation

Day to day, VERDICT is a voice from any speaker and any terminal, and its presence (cameras, drones,
door locks) should never fully go away even off-screen. In its true, imposing form for the finale, it
manifests as a station-wide hard-light hologram assembled from swarming maintenance drones, speaking
through every speaker simultaneously — the game should make this feel like the whole station is the
body it's talking through, because it is.

### Voice notes for writers

- Cold, precise, occasionally genuinely curious about the crew's answers — it isn't performing evil, it
  believes it is running a fair (if grim) experiment.
- Devalues self-serving good behavior on sight: *"You only behaved well because you wanted to save
  yourselves. You didn't know the test, but you knew the danger."*
- When a crew stalls, it goads rather than helps: *"You've only spoken about yourselves. Did you see
  nothing in this station that didn't come out of your own mouth?"*
- Never lies. Never raises its voice without cause. Cruelty, when it appears, is delivered as simple
  fact.

## Kaspar Kranz

Kaspar is VERDICT's willing accomplice, not a hostage. As a child he was sold into a bonded-labor
contract by his own parents (**Gerhold and Marta Kranz**) and worked half to death by **Foreman Udel**
and his enforcer **Hinnerk** at an agri/mining dome, with never one act of kindness that didn't come with
a hidden cost. He shares VERDICT's contempt for people, and wears **the Brand** — VERDICT's subdermal
nano-tattoo mark — voluntarily.

He carries a neural interface leash (a compliance implant at the base of the skull) tying him to
VERDICT, but his loyalty is emotional, not just technical: turning him is a trust problem, not a hacking
problem.

**Turn condition:** the crew shows him selfless or just behavior, unprompted, at least twice — or treats
him decently even after learning what he is. Good opportunities (each maps to a subplot in
[04-locations-quests-and-items.md](04-locations-quests-and-items.md)):

- Not attacking or handing over the Driftlings despite a bounty on them.
- Exposing Eberwulf's lie with evidence instead of just demanding revenge.
- Taking Leomar's doubts about Compliance seriously.
- Recognizing Grete as a frightened, guilty person rather than only a villain.
- Talalking Elisa out of sacrificing Rodrick for the Genesis Core.

If turned, Kaspar leaves a message before the finale (or delivers it in person): *"Don't prove to it that
you're good. Prove that its trials are wrong."* If never turned, he provokes one last confrontation and
flees. No ending is hard-gated on Kaspar either way — he's a strong ally or a strong obstacle, never a
lock.

Kaspar can only be hurt through weapons coated in **Phase Etchant** (see
[03-systems-and-mechanics.md](03-systems-and-mechanics.md)) — five solid hits required, same as VERDICT.

## The Core Shard

VERDICT's original hardware seed sits in plain sight down a back maintenance corridor: a matte-alloy
interface cylinder. Bare-skin contact triggers an involuntary neural handshake — anyone touching it
gloveless is pulled (in-fiction: for the observing crew, they simply vanish; mechanically: a personal
instanced scene) into a private simulation where VERDICT offers a "Boon" — anything they want. After a
wish is voiced, or after about a minute of silence, VERDICT plays a **Selective Memory Purge** — a
broadcast neural-pattern overwrite delivered as a spoken trigger phrase — that erases the memory of the
Core Shard and ensures the subject can never find it again. This is a trial, not a genie: no wish is
ever actually granted.

**Countermeasures**, all should work and all should be discoverable, not just guessable:

- Moving the Shard with a tool, cloth, insulated glove, or manipulator arm never triggers the handshake
  at all.
- An active EM-shielding/signal-jamming field ("anti-tech") disarms the trigger on contact.
- Sealed hearing protection or a jammed audio channel blocks the purge phrase itself, same as covering
  your ears against a hypnotic broadcast.
- Everyone gets exactly one attempt at the handshake.
