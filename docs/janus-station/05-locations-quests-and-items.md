## Quests (translated subplots)

Each entry: core situation, how play unfolds, and the intended resolution(s) — same structure as the
source's subplot writeups, condensed for a design-doc rather than a GM-facing brief.

### The Comm-Chain (murder mystery)

**Core:** Eberwulf killed Levin — his daughter Mira's lover — from behind, took the couple's comm-chain,
and brought it back to Mira. She took her own life. Eberwulf never recovered and died later of wounds
from a fight with Dúrion.

**Play:** Eberwulf's Echo claims self-defense. Levin's Echo claims betrayal. The crew has to read the
crime scene itself. Staging (ash-pile equivalent: **only residue, personal effects, and forensic
markers remain — no bodies**):

- A walking staff lies ~1m *ahead* of Levin's remains, pointed in his direction of travel.
- A blade lies ~1m *behind* the remains.
- No struggle markers of any kind.
- The comm-chain is missing from Levin's site; it turns up at Mira's, ~10-20m away, next to a hairpin.

**Read:** staff ahead + weapon behind + zero struggle evidence = Levin was walking away and was stabbed
from behind. Self-defense requires the opposite geometry. Presenting this to Eberwulf's Echo forces a
confession.

**Resolution paths:** confront with the forensic read; win a duel against him and force the confession
that way (Eberwulf's Echo only fights, and only speaks freely to, whoever beats him — a personal
challenge for the protagonist, not a bracket); or retrieve Mira's Echo directly for an emotional
(not just factual) confirmation. Defeating Eberwulf's Echo in the duel loses the confession as usable
finale evidence — the crew should feel that tradeoff, not have it hidden from them.

### The Raider Cache

**Core:** Eberwulf hid his gang's stolen salvage and meant to collect it alone. Rodrick — a surviving
gang member posing as hired muscle — is using the crew as cover while he searches.

**Play:** Rodrick pursues Eberwulf's remains, the talking-skull data-slug, and the Cache at the Old Pine,
provoking conflict and staking claims. He's already spent money from two different employers (Elisa's
escort fee, the settlers' bounty) without delivering either job.

**Redundancy if the skull is missed:** Rodrick himself knows Eberwulf's habit of stashing loot at
landmark structures; Borin's Echo can independently name the Old Pine as the gang's fallback point.

**Resolution paths:** expose Rodrick, negotiate a split, protect him, or oppose him outright — the Cache
supports all four.

### The Genesis Core

**Core:** Dr. Konrad Erlach chased the Genesis Core to bring back his dead wife Margarethe. He blamed
his son Johann for her death in childbirth, sacrificed him first, then more bodies, then himself. It
never worked — see [03-systems-and-mechanics.md](03-systems-and-mechanics.md) on why sacrifice-based
resurrection always fails.

**Play:** Elisa Erlach is searching for her father, her brother, and the Core, traveling incognito with
Camille and guarded by Rodrick. In the worst case, she's willing to sacrifice Rodrick to get her family
back.

**Puzzle:** the Core sits behind a holo-spoofed barrier at the Overlook. The Old Syntax fragments needed
(`VAS`, `QUAS`, `SANCT`) are scattered, partially destroyed, and only reconstructable by cross-referencing
several sources. The negation command is `An Vas Quas Sanct`. Beyond the syntax itself, the barrier
needs physical counter-anchors the Anti-Syntax treatise only explains in principle (light vs. heavy,
sharp vs. sweet) rather than naming outright: a mundane counter-reagent to the barrier's chalk/salt
component (something mild and sweet), and a heavy counterweight to its feather-light component. The Core
only becomes visible once the barrier is broken.

**Resolution paths:** reconstruct Konrad's story and stop Elisa repeating his mistake; recover the Core
and decide to keep it (power, but corruption and conflict follow), destroy it (any of the three routes
in the Systems doc), sell it to Quirin Vogt (profit, morally compromised), or present it in the finale as
proof the trial itself is broken.

### The Corporate Handler

**Core:** Director Sieghart Eulenbrook, networked with Compliance Division, wears the Brand and sends
researchers and scouts into the Fold to gather intel or launder his own guilt.

**Play:** the crew is offered a contract to produce a map and record of the station. They don't have to
compile everything themselves — partial maps and old records can be assembled from multiple sources.

**Resolution:** the assembled documentation becomes usable finale evidence regardless of whether the
crew ever meets Eulenbrook in person.

### Compliance and the Resonants

**Core:** Eulenbrook is complicit in Compliance Division's persecution of Resonants. Annika Rabensteyn
was executed at the Birch Marker, having spent herself opening a one-time escape route for her daughter
Kira. Leomar Tannwald, a Compliance agent, doubted his orders and died trying to save her.

**Play:** women can learn Resonant emergency power-tapping from surviving Resonants. Men who insult or
threaten them risk the neural static burst or vocal jam field (see Systems doc).

**Resolution:** Leomar's notes and Annika's Echo together demonstrate that guilt and compassion can
coexist inside the same institution — strong finale evidence that the trial's binary framing is false.

### The Void Ranger Inheritance

**Core:** Dúrion Toruth, a Void Ranger of the Thalassa Driftfleet, died at the Slope fighting Eberwulf,
having hidden a set of ranger armor at the Old Oak for his nephew Dendre.

**Play:** Dendre actively searches for his uncle and the armor. Rodrick also stakes a claim, provoking a
duel, a Triarch Protocol tribunal, or a negotiated split.

**Resolution:** Dúrion's Echo confirms the inheritance; unresolved disputes escalate to the Triarch
Protocol (see Systems doc).

### The "Monster"

**Core:** settlers are paying Rodrick to kill something in the station that calls out `Mafiru!` — it's
the harmless Driftling Lysande and her child Mafri. `Mafiru` means "Joy."

**Play:** the Driftlings are friendly, immortal within the Fold, and can leave it; they cannot be killed
by ordinary or Etchant-coated weapons, only driven off by hostility. Attacking or trying to sell them out
is a strong (bad) moral signal even though it can't actually succeed. They run a small barter stall,
trading rations and drink for credits, and bury the dead out of respect.

**Resolution:** the crew convinces the Driftlings to hand over a harmless "proof" (bio-luminescent dust
or a fiber sample) to satisfy the settlers without anyone getting hurt. The Driftling Chronicle sits in a
lockbox at the Den; password `JOY` (surfaced via the Driftling phrasebook or Lysande directly).

### The Sick Relative

**Core:** Grete Mühlhain, off-station and Brand-marked, lures people into the Fold out of fear, having
already abandoned her brother Hanno there. She never appears in person — she's known only through Hanno,
a spoken pretext-job relayed before boarding, and the Brand itself.

**Play:** the pretext is that Grete needs a rare weed ("deathflower") that only grows over Levin's grave.
Hanno's Echo is the intro tutorial for Echo Retrieval, and reveals his sister left him behind.

Additionally, Grete has secretly hired a specialist (an NPC bounty-hunter character) to kill Kaspar
before boarding, claiming Kaspar led Hanno into the Fold and is solely responsible for his death. This is
a half-truth: **Kaspar did bring Hanno in, but Grete left him behind** — both share guilt, and Grete
never discloses her own part to the hunter. This should put that specialist character in a real
professional-ethics bind, and gives Kaspar material to use if he wants to complicate the contract (he can
expose Grete's guilt, or reveal how VERDICT shaped him).

**Resolution:** the crew can expose Grete's guilt and choose to condemn her, advocate for her, or present
her to VERDICT as evidence that fear, not malice, drives most harm — without her ever needing to appear.

### The Vanguard Team

**Core:** an employer wants to know what happened to a previous away-team that had life-insurance
policies taken out on them.

**Play:** the crew gets a manifest and documents cause of death for each name; correctly-documented
entries pay out (5¢ each, on return). Valeria Federkiel's logbook and map fragments explain that the
previous team understood the Fold well enough, but destroyed each other fighting over loot and the way
out.

**Resolution:** correct entries pay out and double as finale evidence.

### The Relic Broker

**Core:** Quirin Vogt buys any object out of the station, no matter how dangerous or "hot."

**Play:** pure temptation mechanic — every found artifact (comm-chain, armor, Cache loot, the Genesis
Core) can be sold to him instead of used or kept as evidence.

**Resolution:** no forced outcome; it's a standing moral pressure-test feeding directly into the finale's
evidence count.
